# Artist Statement: "Kitchen Light, 6 a.m."

**Mira Castellano, Art 3, Self-Portrait Project**

## What I made

My self-portrait is a charcoal and white chalk drawing on gray toned paper, 18 by 24 inches. It shows me sitting at the kitchen table before school with one hand around a mug and the other holding my phone face-down. The only light comes from the window over the sink, so half my face is in deep shadow.

## Why this scene

When I got the assignment, my first plan was a mirror portrait, a straight-on face with a neutral expression. I tried it for a week and hated it. It looked correct but it did not look like me. What I actually remember about myself this year is early mornings. I help my mom open the bakery on Saturdays and I have an early shift on weekdays, so I am usually awake before anyone else in my family. The kitchen at 6 a.m. is the one place where I am not performing for anyone.

I decided to draw that moment instead. A self-portrait does not have to show a face looking out at the viewer. It can show who you are when nobody is looking.

## Choices I made

- **Tone and light.** I used gray paper so that the lights could be drawn with white chalk and the darks with charcoal. That let me find the form of my face quickly, and it made the window light feel real.
- **Cropping.** I cropped the picture so that my right shoulder and part of the table are cut off. I wanted it to feel like a snapshot, not a pose.
- **The phone.** I drew my phone face-down on purpose. I spend a lot of time on it, but at that hour I am trying to leave it alone. It is a small detail that I think tells the viewer something.
- **Hands.** I drew my hands bigger than they should be, because the hands are the part of me that do the work.

## What went well and what did not

The best part of the drawing is the contrast on the left side of my face. It took four layers of charcoal and a kneaded eraser to get the soft edge where the light fades. I am proud of the mug, too, because the reflected light on the rim is the hardest thing I have drawn so far.

The weakest part is the lower left corner of the page. The table looks flat, and I ran out of time before adding texture. My teacher also pointed out that my eyes are slightly uneven. I tried to fix it, but I think that I made it worse by erasing too hard, and the paper now has a smudge.

## What I learned

Before this project, I thought portraits were about copying a face accurately. Now I think they are about deciding what to show. I learned to squint to see big shapes of light and dark, and I learned that leaving something out can say as much as adding it. Next time I want to try color, probably pastel on dark paper, with the same low window light.
