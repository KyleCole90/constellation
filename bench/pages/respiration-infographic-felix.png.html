How your cells make ATP

Cellular respiration in three steps · Felix Aarons · Biology
C6H12O6 + 6 O2 → 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + ATP
1

Glycolysis

Where: cytoplasm

One glucose (6 carbons) is split into two pyruvate (3 carbons each).

No oxygen needed.

net +2 ATP
2

Krebs cycle

Where: mitochondrial matrix

Pyruvate is broken down and CO2 is released (this is the CO2 you breathe out).

Loads up electron carriers NADH and FADH2.

+2 ATP
3

Electron transport chain

Where: inner mitochondrial membrane

Electrons from NADH and FADH2 pass down a chain. Oxygen is the final acceptor and makes water.

The energy pumps H+ ions, which drive ATP synthase.

about +32 to 34 ATP

Without oxygen? Cells can only do glycolysis and then fermentation, so they make just 2 ATP. That is why your muscles burn when you sprint.
Plants do this too! Plants make glucose in photosynthesis, but they still need mitochondria to break it down into ATP.
