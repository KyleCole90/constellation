StudentTessa Bryant
SiteHollis Park pond, east shore
DatesOct 2, Oct 9, Oct 16
StatusCompleteReflection added

Site description

The pond is about 40 meters across with a muddy east shore and a gravel path on the west. Cattails grow along the north edge and the water is shaded by two willows. On all three visits the weather was cool and partly cloudy.

💡 Question: Does the number of species change between the shaded north edge and the open east shore?

Observation log

Date Location Organism Count Notes
Oct 2 North edge Green frog 3 Dove in when I got within 1 m
Oct 2 North edge Water strider 14 Clustered in a sunny patch
Oct 2 East shore Mallard duck 5 2 females, 3 males
Oct 9 East shore Dragonfly (blue) 2 Landed on cattail stalk
Oct 9 North edge Snail (pond) 9 On underside of lily pad
Oct 16 East shore Canada goose 7 Loud; flew off at 4:40 pm

Water measurements

Temperature (thermometer, 5 cm depth): Oct 2, 16 °C. Oct 9, 14 °C. Oct 16, 11 °C. pH strip: 7.5 each time. The water got clearer as it cooled.

Reflection

I expected more variety on the sunny east shore, but the shaded north edge had more species overall: frogs, snails and insects. My guess is that the cattails and lily pads give shelter. I only visited three times, so I would need more data before I could say this is a pattern. Next time I would count at the same hour each visit and photograph each spot so I can compare.

  • Bring a net and a second thermometer
  • Learn to identify the grasses
  • Ask Mr. Okafor about the snails
