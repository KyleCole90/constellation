The mill that made Riverbend

For 106 years, the sound of the Riverbend Woolen Mill set the rhythm of the town. This exhibit follows the people, the machines and the river behind it.

Start the timeline

Welcome

I made this exhibit for U.S. History because my great-grandfather, Anselmo Vega, worked at the mill from 1921 until it closed. When I started asking questions I found out that almost everyone in our town has someone who worked there.

Here you will find a timeline of the mill, profiles of four workers I interviewed or researched, photographs from the county historical society, and a page of sources. Use the menu above to move around.

Why does a small wool mill matter? Because it shows, in one place, what industrialization did to an ordinary American town: it brought jobs and a company store, new families and new problems, strikes and, eventually, the end of an era.

Quick facts

Founded: 1868
Peak workforce: about 900 (1923)
Product: wool blankets and army cloth
Power: water wheel, then steam, then electric
Closed: 1974
