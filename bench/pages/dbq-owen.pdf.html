Did Industrialization Help Workers?

The Gilded Age was a time from about 1870 to 1900 when the United States changed a lot. Many new inventions like the telephone and the light bulb were created and big factories were built. People moved from farms to cities to find jobs and many immigrants came from Europe. In this essay I will talk about how industrialization affected workers and I will argue that it helped them some but also hurt them.

First, industrialization gave people jobs. Before the factories, most people were farmers and if the crops failed they had no money. In the factories workers got paid every week. Document A shows that wages went up over time, from $380 in 1870 to $490 in 1900. That means that people had more money to spend. This is a way industrialization helped workers.

Also, products got cheaper. Because of the assembly line and mass production, things like shoes, clothes and tools cost less. This helped workers because their money went further. Andrew Carnegie also helped people by giving money to build libraries. In Document B he says that rich people should use their money to help others. Over 2,500 libraries were built with his money.

However, industrialization also hurt workers. Document C is a photo of children working in a factory. They are very young and look tired. Child labor was a big problem in the Gilded Age because factory owners wanted to pay as little as possible. Children could not go to school if they were working so they did not get an education. This is a way industrialization hurt workers.

Another problem was the hours. Document D is from the Knights of Labor and it says they wanted an eight hour day. This means that workers were working a lot more than that, maybe twelve hours. They also worked in dangerous places and there were no laws for safety. A lot of workers got hurt or even killed. Because of this, workers started unions like the Knights of Labor and later the American Federation of Labor to fight for better conditions.

There were also strikes. In the Great Railroad Strike of 1877 workers stopped working after wage cuts and it spread across many states. The government sent in troops to stop it. Later there was the Homestead Strike in 1892 and the Pullman Strike in 1894. These strikes show that workers were not happy with how they were treated. Sometimes the strikes worked, but a lot of the time the owners won.

Another thing is that cities got very crowded. Many workers lived in tenements which were small apartments with no windows and bad air. Diseases like tuberculosis and cholera spread quickly. Jacob Riis wrote a book called How the Other Half Lives in 1890 to show people how bad it was. This also affected the lives of workers a lot.

In conclusion, industrialization helped workers by giving them jobs, raising wages and making products cheaper. But it also hurt them with child labor, long hours, dangerous conditions and crowded cities. I think that it helped some people and hurt other people, so it was both good and bad. The Gilded Age was a very important time in American history because it made the United States into an industrial power, and the problems that workers had led to reforms in the Progressive Era.
